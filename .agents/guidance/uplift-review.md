# Independent uplift review and repair

Use for stage C of initial uplift with [TLC](TLC/uplift.md) or [Verus](Verus/uplift.md). This is the uplift's own gap-closure loop, not an automatic invocation of a separate comprehensive review or production-repair workflow. Preserve the task's scope and permissions.

## One round: discover, adjudicate, repair, validate

1. Give fresh reviewers the scope, source inventory and originals, actual ontology bodies, models, implementation, bindings and raw evidence. Have them first derive important behaviors and cases from governing sources and inspected operations, then compare those expectations with the ontology and models, before reading the author's defenses or resolved-finding list. Split coherent domains when helpful, with a reviewer covering cross-domain interactions. Different providers are optional; changing providers alone does not establish independence.
2. Retain material findings with source/owner, trigger, discrepancy and limits. Adjudicate against governing evidence: reviewer proposals are not automatically requirements. Distinguish missing or incorrect intent, model errors, correspondence errors, product defects, unresolved decisions and missing/stale evidence. Keep justified dismissals and their reasons alongside accepted findings.
3. Delegate repairs to A/B workers or fresh repair agents with disjoint ownership. Correct the actual owning ontology body, model, binding, coverage record and finding status as appropriate; a corrected report alone leaves an incorrect contract in place. Repair production code or add runtime tests only when authorized.
4. Recheck affected semantics and configurations, including relevant controls and valid completions. Have a reviewer examine consequential repairs and integrate them before the next round. Persist findings, dispositions, checked revisions and remaining gaps in the plan/evidence.

Default to at most **three rounds across the whole uplift**, including resumptions. Several domain reviewers and repair workers can participate in one round; they do not each receive a separate three-round budget. Targeted validation of that round's repairs belongs to the same round, not an unlimited new discovery campaign. Later rounds review the integrated artifacts beyond just the known finding list. Stop early after a clean independent review only when feasible required work is complete. The last round's repairs still require validation. At the cap, report unresolved gaps and incomplete work; do not reset the counter or silently start a fourth round. An explicit user instruction may change the limit.

Prioritize material unmodeled behavior, partial guarantees and suspect abstractions over additional variants of already well-supported checks. Give each delegate a bounded review question and reserve time for repair and validation. Share models and evidence across related obligations; reuse applicable checks rather than rerunning every historical configuration each round.

A clean review means no unresolved material defect in the uplift artifacts or missing required investigation/checks. Faithfully documented product defects and explicit conditional policy choices may remain; they do not require unauthorized repairs to make the review clean.

## What reviewers must challenge

**Every review round must independently assess both coverage and fidelity.** Coverage asks which important source-supported behaviors are missing or only partly represented in the ontology and models. Fidelity asks whether the statements, model transitions and claimed results accurately reflect governing intent and the implementation. Do not defer either assessment to a later round or limit it to validating the previous round's repairs. Review priorities may change, but both assessments must appear in each round's findings and dispositions.

The [ontology, model and implementation review brief](ontology-model-review.md) can be used within this round.

- **Intent and coverage:** consult the full source inventory, including unresolved and newly discovered evidence. Compare source and operation inventories with actual contract clauses, conditions and exceptions. Challenge unjustified exclusions, including behavior labeled “stateless,” “presentation,” “structural” or provisional Q. Check unsupported requirements and missed implications as well as omissions.
- **Model coverage:** compare independently derived behaviors with actual actions and assertions, not model titles. Look inside already-covered features for conditions that change the outcome: empty or mixed inputs, distinct owners/identities, repeated operations, refusal, partial failure and recovery where relevant. Identify the missing case and governing source; mark partial coverage honestly rather than inventing requirements or treating a linked feature as fully checked.
- **Model fidelity and usefulness:** apply the selected [TLC model-checking mechanics](TLC/model-checking.md) or [Verus verification guidance](Verus/verification.md) on correspondence, model review and alternatives to retained and new checks. Trace consequential actions to exact operations, guards, storage constraints, value transformations and commit boundaries. Challenge both passing models and counterexamples: can the required path actually occur, what relevant broken behavior does the assertion exclude, and can production actually execute the alleged failing trace? Check witnesses and broken controls against the configured bounds and enabled actions. An abstraction that removes a protection can invent a bug; one that precomputes the desired outcome can hide it. Consider a better representation or behavioral alternative under the same governing obligations, keeping repairs distinct from simplifications.
- **Evidence:** apply the [TLC](TLC/setup_foundation.md#execution-evidence-and-reuse) or [Verus](Verus/setup_foundation.md#execution-evidence-and-reuse) evidence and reuse rules to raw results, fingerprints and current applicability, including the runner's rejection cases.
- **Findings and simplifications:** account for every material bug and opportunity, including rejected or partial proposals. Check the repair-proposal requirements in stage B of the [TLC](TLC/uplift.md#b-model-behavior-and-connect-it-to-implementation) or [Verus](Verus/uplift.md#b-model-behavior-and-connect-it-to-implementation) uplift and the model guide's behavioral and unmodeled design comparisons.

## Finish the contract people will read

Read the actual ontology bodies as a person rebuilding the major functionality. Check correctness, qualification scope, cohesion, understandable language, one owner per commitment and useful links. Keep evidence detail outside the governing prose unless it changes the contract. Do this semantic pass before format, metadata and link validation; a structural checker cannot establish intent completeness.

Report coverage against a declared inventory, with partial/unmodeled cases and discovery limits. Do not turn “all listed items covered” into a claim that every requirement has been discovered. Known product defects can coexist with a completed uplift when faithfully documented and checked; missing required investigation or checks cannot. Keep conditional results and repair proposals distinguishable from adopted requirements and established guarantees.
