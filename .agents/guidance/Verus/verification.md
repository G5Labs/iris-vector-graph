# Verify a contract with Verus

Use within the selected uplift, downlift or PR scope. Start with the product obligation and the evidence needed to decide it. A pure calculation can use a function precondition and postcondition. A lifecycle with competing writers usually needs a state machine. A cross-service guarantee also needs a protocol and the contracts of its participants.

## Contract and implementation

1. Derive the required result from the governing source. State who acts, on which identity or units, when the operation must succeed or refuse, and which effects a consumer may observe. If two histories have the same current display values but require different outcomes, make both representable. For a rule shared across modules, trace each local contract back to the governing rule or its authorized exception. Identify the operations that can change the facts it reads and how each preserves it. Reuse a shared rule where the semantics agree. Compare a legitimate completion with a case that should be refused or behave differently. Keep accepted requirements, observed behavior and proposed repairs distinct.
2. At L1 express the business rule independently of today's code. At L2 represent the actual data, initialization, command inputs, guards, reads, writes and results. Split transitions at real commit, delivery or interruption boundaries. Include competing writers, retry, replay and alternate readers where they change the outcome. Inspect the producer behind a decisive premise such as “current” or “committed”. Derive it from inputs, or name the assumption and who must establish it. Where a fact can be missing, late or stale, keep “not known” distinct from a known empty or negative value, and take the fallback or refusal from the rule that governs that consumer.
3. Define how L2 state, operations, results and visible effects map to L1. Prove that initial states satisfy the contract and each in-scope L2 step preserves its invariants and implements an allowed L1 step. Justify any stutter against the observable contract. Preserve the supported input domain, permitted success paths and required refusals. Remove a decisive guard or effect in a control and check that the intended property detects the change. Investigate a surviving control for equivalence, a weak contract or an incorrect binding. A build failure, timeout, resource limit or unrelated proof failure gives no verdict on the control.
4. At L3 identify the actual executable body and path used by the product. For supported Rust code, verify that body and establish its callers' premises, build linkage and unchecked edges. For other languages or unverified code, identify the transcription and bind each relevant action to the source operation. When authorized, replay real inputs and compare the permitted outcomes with observations at the actual handler, persistence boundary and final consumer. Use the proved definitions or establish the relation to the executable model used for replay. Derive expected outcomes independently of the implementation. Compare persisted and external effects as well as the return value, including on refusal. Where model inputs come from the product, exercise the producer through to the consumer. A hand-built trace can check the adapter but supplies no observation of the product. For a substituted component or injected fault, establish whether its behavior and ordering are possible in production. Keep any product conclusion that depends on an unconfirmed premise conditional. Change an observed field that decides the outcome and check that replay detects it. Inspection, native tests and finite trace agreement each have a narrower scope than an executable-body proof.

## Composition and exploration

Use the pinned G5Labs/bank-of-ziniu Verus toolchain and record its revision, solver and dependency locks. Retain compatible pins on an existing foundation. If the required tools or libraries are unavailable, record which checks could not run. For foundations using the consolidated libraries, pin the selected G5Labs/actors revision and component provenance in `SOURCE.json`. Read the interface, validation scope and trust assumptions of the modules you use. Instantiate the actor contract for the actual participants, permitted schedules, shared state, messages and ownership. Prove the joint invariant or record the open interference or delivery premise. Local refinement alone leaves the composition claim open.

Use `pgspec` for supported PostgreSQL statement shapes and isolation settings. Compare the environment contract with the actual engine, configuration and queries, including outcomes the system is free to choose. An observation outside the permitted set refutes the model for that configuration. Also check that it rules out a known impossible outcome. A contract that permits every result can pass a differential suite. SQL Server or a different provider needs its own evidence. Use the target's validated adapter or record the boundary as conditional. Reuse verified collection or protocol lemmas when their preconditions hold. Requirements about database choice, lock strategy and service layout come from the project.

Use the explicit-state explorer for suitable finite machines and counterexample traces. Explore the proved definitions, or establish how the explored executable machine corresponds to them. Record the initial states, domains, enabled actions, bounds, reductions, checked properties and whether exploration completed. Distinguish safety, reachability, deadlock and progress. “From every state some continuation can recover” allows an execution that retries forever. For a requirement that *every fair execution* recovers, state which actions are fair and prove the temporal obligation or use a checker that evaluates it. With a weaker available check, record the stronger requirement as open. When replacing an inherited check, compare the same behavior, assumptions and observations, including the old faulty variant.

## Interpret a result

Record the exact theorem or property, its premises and the behavior its control changed. Keep unchecked declarations and external bodies visible in the trust inventory. Inspect proof dependencies, including lemmas imported from pinned libraries, for `assume`, `admit`, `external_body` and omitted targets. A claim that depends on an unchecked boundary is conditional on that boundary. An admission or assumption of the claim itself leaves the proof open. Review changes to trusted code and identify who discharges each remaining premise. Classify expected counterexamples as evidence about the stated design variant.

When Verus fails to prove a claim, record what remains open. To report a model violation, exhibit a permitted input or execution that contradicts the property. To report a product defect, also establish that the violating path is reachable in the actual product. A proof timeout or resource limit supplies neither result. Do not close the gap by narrowing the statement, adding a premise the product does not establish or moving the failing code behind a trusted boundary.

When comparing a proposed simplification, hold the required outcomes, input domain and operating conditions fixed. Show the distinguishing case and how permitted use survives. Then record the formal result, product connection and disposition using the [foundation handoff](setup_foundation.md).
