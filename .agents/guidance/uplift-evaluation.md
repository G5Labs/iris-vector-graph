# Evaluate changes to the uplift harness

Use when an evaluation is requested, not as a gate on every uplift. Distinguish a focused exercise of model/ontology review from a full uplift: a small exercise cannot measure source discovery across a large repository, orchestration, resumptions or overall cost.

Freeze the same source revision, governing inputs, permissions and starting artifacts for every arm. Save the exact harness variants, common prompt, tool/model versions and resource limits before running workers. Give each worker only its allowed inputs in a fresh context; keep evaluator expectations, other arms and prior findings out of its packet. Use the same model/effort and comparable budgets when isolating the effect of guidance. Respect the full workflow when evaluating orchestration; do not disable delegation then claim to have evaluated it.

Derive the evaluator's expected obligations and cases from the original sources and code before inspecting outputs. Independently adjudicate discovered omissions and alleged defects against that evidence. Score concrete supported findings and corrected artifacts, including incorrect extra requirements and claims broader than their evidence. A keyword, model count or author-reported pass is not credit. Keep stable denominators and disclose newly discovered obligations separately. Preserve raw outputs and ambiguous cases so another reviewer can reproduce the judgment.

Compare recovered intent, missing behavior inside linked features, unsupported requirements, model correctness/usefulness, model-to-code correspondence, propagation of corrections, readable contract reconstruction and actual checks executed. Include at least one case where a passing model is too weak, one where an abstraction invents a bug, and one where a useful alternative preserves the governing behavior. Measure elapsed time and actual available token/tool usage; label unavailable telemetry and do not infer cost from prose length. For whole-workflow trials, include an interrupted/resumed case and check the source ledger and global review-round counter.

Report the scope, per-arm outcomes, resource differences, repetitions and limits. A single seeded exercise may expose regressions or a ceiling effect; it cannot establish general superiority or a causal improvement in real-project uplift quality. Replicate on matched representative repositories before making that stronger claim. Retain poor or tied results rather than changing the rubric after seeing them. Evaluation changes improve the evaluation; they do not retroactively improve a candidate's score.
