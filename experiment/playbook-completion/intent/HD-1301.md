# HD-1301 Should the redaction's opening check and its marker be one locked step?

Question ([original](../../playbook-uplift/DECISIONS.md)): Adopt the reviewed design (Start checks liveness and the marker under the head lock and sets the marker in its own transaction), closing F-1302 and F-1303? A no-op redaction would then write its marker and remove it; the audit revision is written only when something was redacted.

## Contradictions

- `ADR-0013:18; Ledger.cls:514 ('while a redaction runs, nothing may be written into its target')` vs `plan.md:23; LedgerRedact.cls:322-324 (marker only at the first tombstone)`: Between redact()'s unlocked liveness check and its first committed tombstone, a commit into the target is accepted and later tombstoned while live (F-1302, live in the original run).
- `ADR-0013:18 ('A redaction of a different target is refused until the first one finishes')` vs `LedgerRedact.cls:202-205 (unlocked marker read), 322-324, 439`: Two runs of different targets that start before either sets the marker both run; their audits mix (F-1303, live in the original run).
- `spec.md:125 FR-006 / spec.md:88 ('takes the head lock like a commit')` vs `LedgerRedact.cls:187-212 (refusal checks and PrePass take no lock)`: The spec puts the whole redaction under the head lock discipline; the opening checks run outside it.

## As built

Opening checks (other-target marker, liveness) are read without the head lock; the marker is set by the first tombstone's batch; only the final step re-checks liveness under the lock, after tombstones are committed, and then refuses with target_live leaving the marker (a stuck run). Live in this lane: a replayed retry writes into the target while the marker exists (ib-HD1305-retry-revives, part 2), a second route around the commit refusal.

Evidence: inspected `iris_src/src/Graph/KG/LedgerRedact.cls:187-216, 322-324, 409-441`; live `original 9c3719c1 scenarios commit_in_unmarked_window, two_targets_at_once`; live `ib-HD1305-retry-revives`

## Intended

Status: **assumption**. Outcome supported (ADR-0013:18, Ledger.cls:514 comment, FR-005/006, e4b5eef5): for the whole run, from the call, commits into the target are refused, a different target's run is refused, and a record whose content is live is never tombstoned. The specific mechanism (liveness check and marker in one locked step at start) is the best-supported reading, not a maintainer decision; the maintainer's own plan prescribes the late marker.

## Working assumption

Adopt the reviewed design: Start = one transaction under the head lock that checks the other-target marker, checks liveness, and sets the marker; batches as now; Final re-checks and removes the marker; a no-op run writes and removes its marker and writes no audit revision (as FinalInLock:418 already does). Pair it with HD-1201 (erasure holds the head lock) and HD-1305 (a replayed retry applies nothing), otherwise the locked start is bypassed by an open erasure or by a replay.

Changed from decisions.md: **no**. Same choice. Added the maintainer's own statement of the outcome (Ledger.cls:514 comment) and two couplings from this lane's live probes: the open-erasure counterexample (ib-HD1201-open-erasure) would, by reasoning over the model, also defeat a locked start unless the erasure holds the lock; and a replayed retry writes into a marked target, observed live (ib-HD1305-retry-revives).

## Alternatives

- (a) one locked Start step (check + marker), reviewed design
- (b) target-specific ownership (per-target marker and lock) that still preserves the global reader and audit promises
- (c) keep the late marker and document the window (contradicts ADR-0013:18)

## Residual question for the maintainer

Is a busy/refused answer acceptable for commits into a target from the moment redact() is called, including a no-op run that sets and removes its marker? Working answer: yes. Best alternative: per-target ownership.

Consequence rank hint: high (live content's history destroyed irreversibly; tenant blocked from ledger writes until re-erased; audit misattributed)
