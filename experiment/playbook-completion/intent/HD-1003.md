# HD-1003 Should the shortest-path routines refuse a direction they do not walk?

Question ([original](../../playbook-uplift/decisions/10.md)): `TraversalPaths.ShortestPathJson` walks only "out" and "both" and answers no path for anything else; `ShortestLength` reads any value but "both" as "out". Callers turn "in" round. `DijkstraJson` and `DijkstraCost` refuse through `CheckDirection`. Refuse (or implement) at the class level, or keep the guard at the callers?

## Contradictions

- `commit 99b00ce9 message (every ObjectScript entry point refuses a direction outside its set)` vs `TraversalPaths.cls:5-80 (ShortestPathJson) and :117-122 (ShortestLength) accept any string`: The 4.6.0 round-3 policy was not applied to the two shortest-path class methods.
- `iris_sql_store.py:2163-2167 (shortest_path_length docstring)` vs `live ib-HD1003-path-direction`: The public store method answers 1 for (x, y, 'in') while execute_shortest_path(x, y, 'in') answers no path.

## As built

Edge x->y. ShortestPathJson(y,x,'in') and (y,x,'sideways') answer [] silently. ShortestLength(x,y,'in') and (x,y,'sideways') answer 1, a false positive, and ShortestLength(y,x,'in') answers -1. DijkstraJson refuses 'sideways'. IRISGraphStore.shortest_path_length(x,y,'in') answers 1, but execute_shortest_path(x,y,'in') answers none. The engine's Cypher (y)<-[*..5]-(x) is correct (length 1) through the caller guard.

Evidence: live `ib-HD1003-path-direction`

## Intended

Status: **supported**. A routine must not answer an unsupported direction as 'no path' or as another direction's path. It refuses a direction outside its set or implements it; this is maintainer policy in 99b00ce9. Whether the class methods implement 'in' or refuse it is open. Python store methods carry the public semantics, and their docstrings make shortest_path_length agree with execute_shortest_path.

## Working assumption

Keep the public 'in' semantics, caller reversal included. Require ShortestPathJson and ShortestLength to refuse any direction outside {out, both}, or to implement 'in' by walking from the far end. Require IRISGraphStore.shortest_path_length to reverse 'in' as execute_shortest_path does. Record the live false positive as a defect against its docstring.

Changed from decisions.md: **yes**. Status moves from assumption to supported: maintainer commit 99b00ce9 states the refuse-outside-set policy for ObjectScript entry points. New live evidence shows a wrong positive answer, not just a missing one, on a public store method.

## Alternatives

- (a) CheckDirection in both class methods and 'in' walked from the far end
- (b) keep the guard at the callers and document the class methods as internal (conflicts with 99b00ce9)
- refuse 'in' at the class level and reverse in every Python caller, including shortest_path_length

## Discriminating check

probe (run): `experiment/playbook-completion/intent/probes/lane-b/hd1003_path_direction.py; ib-HD1003-path-direction`. Result: fail: silent [] for 'in'/'sideways' on ShortestPathJson; ShortestLength and the public IRISGraphStore.shortest_path_length answer 1 where no path exists against the edges; Dijkstra refuses.

## Confidence and what would change it

Confidence: high. A statement that TraversalPaths' shortest-path methods are internal and exempt from the 99b00ce9 policy. Even then, the store method's docstring discrepancy remains.
